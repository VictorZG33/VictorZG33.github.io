About

I am a PhD candidate in Chemical Engineering at Yale University, in Shu Hu’s Photocatalysis and Reaction Engineering Lab, which I joined in 2021. My work sits between semiconductor processing and electrochemistry. I develop protective and passivating coatings for III–V semiconductors such as InP and GaInP, do the nanofabrication that turns them into devices, and write the transport models that connect a recipe to a result.

Before Yale I studied chemical engineering at the Cooper Union and at Columbia University, where my research followed ion transport in membrane-coated electrocatalysts.

What I work on

Atomic layer deposition
Process development for TiO2, Al2O3 and GaN films and multilayer stacks, tuned for passivation and interface chemistry. I also run and maintain the group’s plasma ALD system.
Nanofabrication
Electron-beam and photolithography, reactive ion etching and PECVD for sub-100 nm III–V structures, checked with SEM/EDX, XPS, AFM and ellipsometry.
Modeling
COMSOL and ANSYS Fluent models of transport, reaction and plasma, used to plan experiments and to explain their results. One example is the coupled transport and pore-diffusion model in our paper on electrified polyethylene pyrolysis.
Solar fuels
Photoelectrochemistry and photocatalysis on coated semiconductors, from water splitting to turning dissolved carbon into fuels.
